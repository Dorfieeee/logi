# Community, clans, and players

## Community directory

The **Community** page brings together discoverable players, clans, and recent public matches. Use it to explore rather than to administer a server. Long lists are split across pages; use the page controls to continue browsing.

Choose a game before browsing the directory. The selected game scopes player
search, clan search, clan browsing, and public match history; the player and
clan search run together from one search field. Clan lists and search results
are split into pages—use **Load more** to continue. Records created before game
support have no stored game ID and are treated as **Hell Let Loose**, so they
appear whenever Hell Let Loose is selected.

## Clan pages

A clan page presents public identity, active-member count, and recorded match
history. It does not expose the member list itself. A clan is eligible for the
directory once Logi sees at least one active assignment or recorded member ID.
A newly created, empty workspace therefore will not appear. Competition teams
come from Logi's global team catalogue and are not clans in this directory.

Clan match statistics use only non-training events with a linked imported match
record. Win rate counts only victory/defeat decisions; draws do not count as a
win or loss decision. The page shows a limited recent-match list, not an
exhaustive operational history.

The top of a clan page shows the clan's logo, name, description, the games it
plays and its active-member count. **Join on Discord** appears when a clan
manager has added the clan's Discord invite (Settings › Clan profile); it only
ever opens a `discord.gg` invite. Below are the clan's matches, wins and win
rate, and its place in a published competition when one of its catalogue teams
plays there (no place is shown before the division has a result).

**Upcoming** lists the clan's next announced matches: matches (never trainings)
that are not drafts, whose announcement time has passed and that have not
started. Only the start time, the game, the opponent team from the match's team
selection (or the match name), and the competition or category are shown—never
the description, notes, server or password, sign-ups or player names.
**Recent matches** shows the latest recorded results from the clan's side:
win/loss, score, opponent, map and date.

A clan page presents the clan’s public identity and relevant public activity. It may link to its players and matches. Information is only as current as the community’s Logi records and connections.

## Player profiles

A player page is created only when the player has at least one qualifying
non-training match-stat record linked to an event, including a confirmed
Warcon source. The profile includes only
active-clan badges and a limited recent-match/statistical summary; it does not
expose Discord IDs, platform IDs, membership assignments, notes, or unpublished
roster data.

Each clan appears once on a player profile. Its game icons sit side by side
next to its name and represent that player’s active memberships in the clan.
Hover an icon to see the game name.

Use the game links on a player page to switch its statistics. The default is
its most recently played game. Totals, match history, comparisons and graphs
use that game only. Hell Let Loose and Hell Let Loose: Vietnam show kills,
deaths, K/D, offense, defense and support. Wardogs shows kills, deaths, K/D
and **Money** (money earned or lost during recorded matches, not a wallet
balance), with a money
trend instead of HLL effectiveness charts. Missing money is unknown, not zero.
Other games have no gameplay-stat sections until their metrics are defined.
K/D always displays two decimal places, including chart tooltips.

A player page identifies a player and can show their public game or community data. It is not an invitation to edit another person’s account. Players manage their own account settings after signing in; server managers manage server-specific membership separately.

Websites integrating these public statistics can request an exact Discord-account
lookup through the public API with `identity=discord`. This avoids confusion with
historical imported IDs. A unique matching account is required; missing or duplicate
accounts return no profile. The optional response binding lets a website compare
the result with its already-authorized current native identity without receiving
another raw identifier. It does not establish account ownership, clan membership
or consent to publish a personal biography. Existing profile URLs and the default
imported-ID lookup retain their current behavior. See the [API reference](/api-reference)
for the selector and comparison protocol.

<Callout type="info">
    Can’t find a player or clan? It may not be public, it may not have been
    imported or linked yet, or you may need a more specific name.
</Callout>
